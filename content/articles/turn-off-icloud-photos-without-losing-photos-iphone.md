## Which iCloud Photos option should you choose?

The choice depends on whether photos should remain on this iPhone, remain only in iCloud, or leave iCloud entirely.

| Your goal | Best action | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stop syncing but keep full copies on this iPhone | Download originals, turn off **Sync this iPhone**, then choose **Download Photos & Videos** | The iPhone keeps or downloads copies; the iCloud library remains. |
| Stop syncing and remove iCloud-synced copies from this iPhone | Turn off **Sync this iPhone**, then choose **Remove from iPhone** | The synced copies are removed from this iPhone; the iCloud library remains. |
| Keep syncing but use less iPhone storage | Keep iCloud Photos on and use **Optimize iPhone Storage** | Full-resolution originals remain in iCloud; the device can use space-saving versions. |
| Stop iCloud Photos for the whole Apple Account | Choose **Turn Off and Delete Photos** under iCloud storage | Apple schedules the photos and videos stored in iCloud for deletion after 30 days. |

Sources: [1–3].

If you only need more device space, **Optimize iPhone Storage** may help without disconnecting the library. [2]

## Before you turn off iCloud Photos

1. Open **Photos → Collections**, tap your account profile, and check the library status. Resolve unexpected pauses, storage warnings, or incomplete syncing first. [4]
2. Verify important photos and videos. Thumbnails alone do not prove that full originals are stored locally.
3. Check free space under **Settings → General → iPhone Storage**. A large library may not fit on the phone. [2]
4. If local full-resolution copies are the goal, use Apple’s current **Download and Keep Originals** setting and allow time for downloading. [3]
5. Consider a separate verified copy for irreplaceable photos.

Apple specifically says not to turn off iCloud Photos when the library is unexpectedly failing to sync or is paused. Turning it off is not a troubleshooting shortcut; Apple says doing so restarts the syncing process. [4]

## What if Optimize iPhone Storage is on?

With **Optimize iPhone Storage**, full-resolution originals stay in iCloud while the iPhone can keep smaller, space-saving versions when needed. A photo being visible in the Photos app therefore does not establish that its full-resolution original is already stored on the phone. [2][3]

On current iOS 27 documentation, Apple’s route for local originals is **Settings → your name → iCloud → Photos → iPhone Storage → Download and Keep Originals**. Let the library download and confirm that the phone has enough capacity before disconnecting sync. [3]

Downloading can take time. Do not erase another copy until the files you intend to keep are available.

## How to keep photos on the iPhone and stop syncing

Apple’s current one-device process is: [1]

1. Download your original photos and videos, and make sure the iPhone has enough storage.
2. Open **Settings** and tap your name.
3. Tap **iCloud**.
4. Under **Saved to iCloud**, tap **Photos**.
5. Turn off **Sync this iPhone**.
6. Choose **Download Photos & Videos**.

After sync is off on this iPhone, new uploads, edits, and deletions no longer synchronize through iCloud Photos between this phone and the other devices using the library. The iCloud library can continue to exist for those other devices. [1]

## What does Download Photos & Videos do?

This choice tells the iPhone to keep or download copies of the photos and videos stored in iCloud rather than remove them from the device. [1]

The request still needs sufficient storage and time. If optimization was active, full-resolution originals may still need downloading; the button does not guarantee completion regardless of capacity or connection. [1–3]

## What does Remove from iPhone mean?

In the one-device sync-off flow, **Remove from iPhone** removes the iCloud-synced photos from that iPhone without saving a copy locally. The iCloud Photos library remains in the Apple Account and can continue syncing with other enabled devices. [1]

It does **not** mean “delete the entire iCloud library.” It can, however, leave this phone without those local copies.

## Remove from iPhone vs deleting a photo normally

These are different actions.

- **Remove from iPhone** appears when disconnecting this device from iCloud Photos. It removes the synced copies from this phone while leaving the iCloud library in place. [1]
- **Delete** in the Photos app changes the active library. While iCloud Photos remains enabled, deleting a synced photo or video on one device also deletes it from other devices signed in to the same Apple Account with iCloud Photos enabled. [5]

Normally deleted items go to **Recently Deleted** and can generally be recovered for 30 days. That mechanism is separate from the account-wide 30-day period after stopping iCloud Photos. [5]

> **Important:** **Remove from iPhone** in the one-device sync-off workflow is not the same as tapping **Delete** on an individual photo while iCloud Photos is active.

## What does Turn Off and Delete Photos do?

> **Warning — this is account-wide:** Do not choose **Turn Off and Delete Photos** if your goal is only to stop one iPhone from syncing.

Apple places this control under **Settings → your name → iCloud → Storage → iCloud Photos**. It stops iCloud Photos for the account and all devices. Apple says iCloud-stored photos and videos are deleted 30 days after the service is stopped. During that period, the account can use **Undo Delete**. [1]

This does not delete the cloud library immediately. It is also not the same as the **Recently Deleted** album used after normal photo deletion.

## If you only want more iPhone storage, should you turn iCloud Photos off?

Often, no. **Optimize iPhone Storage** keeps full-resolution originals in iCloud while allowing the phone to use smaller versions when needed. [2][3]

Savings vary. Optimization is a storage setting, not an independent backup.

## What happens to new photos after Sync this iPhone is off?

Uploads of new photos, edits, and deletions no longer sync between this iPhone and other devices using iCloud Photos. New pictures therefore do not automatically upload through iCloud Photos while the setting is off. [1]

Do not assume the photo has another cloud copy unless a separate, verified backup or transfer includes it.

## iCloud Photos vs iCloud Backup

**iCloud Photos** continuously syncs the photo library. **iCloud Backup** periodically backs up eligible device information not already syncing to iCloud. [6]

When iCloud Photos is on, synced photos and videos are not included again in the normal daily iCloud Backup. When it is off, iCloud Backup may include locally stored photos and videos, subject to settings, storage, and a successful backup. [6]

Turning off photo sync does not automatically create a safe backup. DECODISTA’s explainer on [whether iCloud Backup includes your photos](</articles/does-icloud-backup-include-photos>) covers the checks to make before relying on a device backup.

## Shared Photo Library warning

Apple’s account-wide instructions say an owner must delete the Shared Photo Library before turning off iCloud Photos for the account. Shared Library has separate organizer, participant, and copy rules. [1][7]

When a Shared Library is deleted, participants who have been members for more than seven days can receive everything in their personal libraries; newer participants receive only their contributions. A participant who leaves can choose everything or only their contributions. Review the rule for your role first. [7]

## Safe checklist before you change anything

- ☐ The Photos library status shows syncing is complete or behaving as expected.
- ☐ Important photos and videos are present where expected.
- ☐ The iPhone has enough free storage for the originals I want locally.
- ☐ I selected **Download and Keep Originals** and allowed downloads to finish when local originals are the goal.
- ☐ I reviewed Shared Photo Library implications for my organizer or participant role.
- ☐ I considered a separate verified copy for irreplaceable photos.
- ☐ I know which action I intend: **Download Photos & Videos**, **Remove from iPhone**, or account-wide **Turn Off and Delete Photos**.

## FAQ

### Can I turn off iCloud Photos without deleting photos?

Yes. Download the originals, confirm enough storage, turn off **Sync this iPhone**, and choose **Download Photos & Videos**. The iCloud library remains. [1–3]

### Which option keeps photos on my iPhone?

Choose **Download Photos & Videos**. If optimization was active, allow time and space for full-resolution originals to download. [1–3]

### Does Remove from iPhone delete photos from iCloud?

Not in this one-device workflow. It removes the iCloud-synced copies from that iPhone without saving them locally; the account’s iCloud Photos library remains. [1]

### What happens if Optimize iPhone Storage is on?

The phone may hold space-saving versions while full-resolution originals remain in iCloud. Download and verify originals before turning off sync if full local copies are your goal. [2][3]

### Does turning off Sync this iPhone affect my other Apple devices?

It disconnects this iPhone from future iCloud Photos changes. Other devices can continue using the account’s library; uploads, edits, and deletions stop syncing between them and this phone. [1]

### What happens to new photos after sync is off?

They no longer upload automatically through iCloud Photos from that iPhone. Protect them through a separate verified backup or transfer if needed. [1]

### Does Turn Off and Delete Photos delete everything immediately?

No. Apple says photos and videos stored in iCloud are deleted 30 days after the service is stopped, and **Undo Delete** is available during the documented period. [1]

### Is iCloud Photos the same as iCloud Backup?

No. iCloud Photos syncs the library. iCloud Backup stores eligible information not already syncing and may include local photos when iCloud Photos is off. [6]

### What if my iCloud Photos are still syncing?

Pause this plan and resolve the sync status first. Apple says not to turn iCloud Photos off when it is failing to sync or unexpectedly paused because switching it off restarts syncing. [4]

### What if I use iCloud Shared Photo Library?

Review your role and the copy rules before changing the account-wide service. Apple requires the owner to delete the Shared Library before account-wide shutdown, while participants have separate leave-and-copy options. [1][7]
