## Is Google Photos Locked Folder backed up?

Not necessarily. Google Photos supports automatic Locked Folder backup, but users can leave it off and manually back up selected items instead. The folder can therefore contain both cloud-backed items and photos or videos stored only on one device. [1][2]

What matters after a lost phone is whether the **specific item successfully backed up**. Its presence in Locked Folder is not proof of backup.

| Situation | Backed-up Locked Folder item | Unbacked Locked Folder item |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phone is lost or damaged | Can remain available on another supported device signed in to the same Google Account | Can be lost with the device |
| Google Photos is uninstalled | Cloud-backed copy remains associated with the account | Google warns the item can be lost |
| Photos app data is cleared | Cloud-backed copy remains associated with the account | Google warns the item can be lost |
| Device is factory-reset | Can be recovered through Locked Folder after sign-in and authentication | Google explicitly warns that locally saved items are deleted by reset |
| Another supported device opens Locked Folder | Available after sign-in and authentication | Does not appear there |
| Google Account storage quota | Counts toward the quota | Does not create a cloud-backed Locked Folder copy |

Sources: [1–3]. “Backed up” in this table means the item completed backup successfully; merely turning a setting on is not the same as verifying the upload.

## Backup on vs backup off

Google requires the main Google Photos backup setting to be on before automatic Locked Folder backup can be enabled. With automatic Locked Folder backup off, a user can still back up selected items manually. One item may therefore be recoverable from the account while another remains device-only. [1][2]

A “Locked Folder backup: off” label does not establish that every item is unbacked. Check the important items before removing the app, clearing its data, resetting the phone or replacing it.

## What happens if you lose or damage your phone?

A backed-up item can be accessed from another supported device signed in to the same Google Account, after the required authentication. An unbacked item is stored only on the original device and can be lost with it. Backup must have completed before the loss; turning on the setting is not itself a recovery guarantee. [1–3]

## What happens after a factory reset?

Google’s current Android and iPhone/iPad help warns that locally saved items with backup off are deleted by factory reset and are not included in ordinary USB-to-USB or cloud device transfers. **Device-only Locked Folder items can be lost after a reset or removal of local app data.** Follow the instructions for the actual platform rather than assuming every transfer method is identical. [1][2]

## What happens if you uninstall Google Photos or clear app data?

Google warns that unbacked items can be lost if Photos is uninstalled or its app data is cleared. Do not use either as a casual troubleshooting step while important device-only content exists. [1][2]

## Important: moving an already-backed-up photo into Locked Folder

> **DECODISTA callout — Moving the item can remove its existing cloud backup.**
>
> If Locked Folder backup is off, Google says moving a photo or video into Locked Folder deletes that item’s Google Photos backup. The item can remain on the device, but it no longer has that previous Photos backup. Turn on Locked Folder backup or manually back up the item if cloud recovery is required. [1][2]

This is different from [Google Photos Undo Backup](</articles/google-photos-android-undo-backup-keep-phone-photos>), which is a separate Android workflow for removing cloud copies while keeping qualifying phone files. Do not substitute one process for the other.

## How to turn Locked Folder backup on

Google’s current mobile steps are: [1][2]

1. Open Google Photos and sign in.
2. Tap the profile picture or initial.
3. Open **Photos settings** on Android or **Google Photos settings** on iPhone/iPad.
4. Tap **Backup → Back up Locked Folder**.
5. Open Locked Folder and authenticate when prompted.
6. Turn **Back up Locked Folder** on.

The main Google Photos backup setting must be enabled first. Check important items after upload rather than assuming the switch completed every backup immediately.

## How to manually back up only selected items

Automatic backup is not all-or-nothing. To back up selected items while automatic Locked Folder backup remains off: [1][2]

1. Open **Collections → Locked** in Google Photos.
2. Authenticate with the device method.
3. Long-press and select the photos or videos.
4. Tap **Back up**.

This backs up the selected items; it does not turn on automatic backup for the whole folder.

## Can you access Locked Folder on another phone or computer?

Backed-up items can appear on another supported phone or tablet signed in to the same account; device-only items cannot. Android uses the device screen lock. iPhone and iPad use Face ID, Touch ID or the device passcode. Google Photos on the web supports backed-up Locked Folder content through Google Account authentication. None offers a different folder-only password. [1–3]

## Locked Folder vs Archive

| Feature | Locked Folder | Archive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authentication | Requires device or Google Account authentication | No separate protected view |
| Search | Hidden from Google Photos search | Archived items can appear in search |
| Albums | Hidden from albums | Archived items can remain in albums |
| Sharing | Item must be moved out before sharing | Not a private-sharing barrier |

Archive is an organization tool, not an equivalent private vault. [1–3]

## How private is Locked Folder?

Google describes Locked Folder as private and not shared, protected by Google Photos’ standard encryption practices. It hides items from the grid, Memories, search, albums and ordinary third-party photo access. This is not a claim of end-to-end or zero-knowledge encryption. Protection depends on the applicable device or Google Account authentication, not an independent folder password. [1–3]

## What happens when you permanently delete something from Locked Folder?

Locked Folder permits permanent deletion and does not allow its contents to be moved to Trash. **Permanent delete in Locked Folder is not the same workflow as deleting a normal Google Photos item to Trash.** Google’s current normal deletion guide gives ordinary Trash items a 30-day retention period, but that period should not be assumed for an item permanently deleted inside Locked Folder. [1–4]

## Why might copies still show outside Locked Folder?

Moving a photo or video moves only the selected item. Copies, edited versions and duplicates can remain elsewhere. If another person already saved or auto-saved a shared copy, changing the original does not change the recipient’s version. Locked Folder is not a recall mechanism. [1–3]

## Does Locked Folder use Google storage?

Backed-up Locked Folder items count toward Google Account storage. Unbacked items remain local rather than creating a cloud-backed Locked Folder copy. [1–3]

If the goal is to recover phone space while retaining the cloud library, that is a different decision. Read [what Google Photos Free Up Space actually deletes](</articles/google-photos-free-up-space-what-it-deletes>) before removing local files.

## Which option matches your goal?

| Your goal | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Keep an item private and recoverable after phone loss | Turn on Locked Folder backup or manually back up that item, then verify it |
| Keep an item only on the current device | Leave that item unbacked and accept the loss, reset and app-data risks |
| Confirm that an important item reached the account | Check that specific item’s backup status before resetting or clearing anything |
| Hide a duplicate or edited version too | Find and move or delete each separate copy deliberately |
| Share a Locked Folder item | Move it out of Locked Folder first |

## Safe checklist before moving important photos

- Decide whether each item should be cloud-backed or device-only.
- Confirm the item’s backup status before deleting, resetting or replacing anything.
- Do not clear Photos app data or uninstall it while important unbacked items remain.
- Do not factory-reset a device before verifying necessary Locked Folder backups.
- Remember that moving a backed-up item can remove its existing Photos backup when Locked Folder backup is off.
- Review duplicates and edited versions outside Locked Folder.
- Assume a recipient’s previously saved copy remains with that recipient.
- Use permanent delete only when recovery is not needed.

## FAQ

### Is Google Photos Locked Folder backed up automatically?

Only if automatic Locked Folder backup is enabled. Google also allows manual backup of selected items while automatic backup is off. [1][2]

### Can I back up only some Locked Folder photos?

Yes. Select individual items inside Locked Folder and tap **Back up**. That does not enable automatic backup for every item. [1][2]

### What happens to Locked Folder photos if I lose my phone?

Backed-up items can remain available through the same Google Account on supported devices. Unbacked items are device-only and can be lost with the phone. [1–3]

### Will a factory reset delete Locked Folder photos?

Google warns that locally stored Locked Folder items with backup off are deleted by factory reset. Successfully backed-up items remain associated with the account. [1][2]

### What happens if I uninstall Google Photos?

Google says unbacked Locked Folder items can be lost. Confirm important backups before uninstalling or clearing app data. [1][2]

### Can I see Locked Folder photos on another phone?

Backed-up items can appear on another supported device signed in to the same account after authentication. Device-only items cannot. [1][2]

### Can I open Locked Folder on a computer?

Yes, for content available through the account. Open Google Photos on the web and choose Locked Folder; Google Account authentication applies. [3]

### Does moving a backed-up photo into Locked Folder delete its cloud backup?

Google says the existing Photos backup is deleted if Locked Folder backup is off. Turn that backup on or manually back up the item if cloud recovery is needed. [1][2]

### Is Locked Folder safer than Archive?

It provides stronger visibility and access controls. Archive items can still appear in search and albums, while Locked Folder requires authentication and hides items from those surfaces. [1–3]

### Can Locked Folder use a separate password?

No. Google says mobile uses the relevant device authentication and the web uses the Google Account password; a different Locked Folder password is not available. [1–3]

### Do deleted Locked Folder photos go to Trash?

No. Google says Locked Folder items cannot be moved to Trash and can be permanently deleted. Do not assume the normal Trash recovery period applies. [1–4]

### Do backed-up Locked Folder photos count toward Google storage?

Yes. Google says backed-up items count toward the Google Account storage quota. [1–3]

### Does moving a photo to Locked Folder hide all copies?

No. Copies, duplicates and edited versions remain where they are unless moved or deleted separately. [1–3]

### Can someone I previously shared a photo with still have their copy?

Yes. If the recipient already saved or auto-saved it, changes to your version—including moving it to Locked Folder—do not change that saved copy. [1–3]
