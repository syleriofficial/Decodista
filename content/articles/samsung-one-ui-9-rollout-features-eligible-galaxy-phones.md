## Privacy Alerts: what they actually do

Samsung describes on-device AI that flags potentially unnecessary access to sensitive information. Examples include excessive precise-location use and background permission access. Alerts are prompts to investigate; Samsung cautions that they may not account for every relevant factor. [R1, R4]

The global announcement’s support footnote explicitly names the following models:

| Family | Models named |
| --- | --- |
| Galaxy S | S26, S26 Ultra; S25, S25 FE; S24, S24 FE; S23, S23 FE |
| Galaxy Z | Fold8 Ultra, Fold8, Flip8, Fold7, Flip7, Fold6, Flip6, Fold5, Flip5 |
| Galaxy Tab | S11 Ultra, S11; S10 Ultra, S10 Plus; S9 Ultra, S9 Plus, S9 |

The global page misspells Fold6 as “Fol6”; Samsung Australia spells out Z Fold6. Australia also includes Tab S12 Ultra and Tab S12 Plus, which the reviewed global and India footnotes omit. Keep those regional differences visible rather than silently combining them into a worldwide promise. [R1, R2, R9]

The footnote does not individually name every Plus, Ultra or Edge variant in older S families. Its omissions establish neither support nor exclusion for those variants. Check exact-model documentation. None of these feature listings proves that an older device has stable One UI 9 available today.

## Security Brief

Security Brief displays information about malware, unknown-source apps and unnecessary permissions inside Now Brief. Samsung’s Australian documentation requires a Samsung Account and says availability depends on country, language, model and apps. Treat it as a place to review security information, not a guarantee that every threat will be detected or a replacement for other protections. [R9]

## AI Assistant Activity

This dashboard records eligible assistant activity: which apps an assistant accessed, the actions it performed and when. Support depends on the assistant, market, device and OS version. Samsung does not promise a complete history of every AI service on every Galaxy. [R4, R5]

The distinction is practical: AI Assistant Activity helps you review an assistant’s actions; Privacy Alerts highlights potentially concerning app access. Neither should be treated as a complete audit of all data leaving a phone.

## Scam Detection expansion

Samsung’s rollout announcement concerns potential **scam calls** and names Japan, Canada and India as expansion markets. Its footnote uses future availability wording and makes support dependent on region, language, model and software version. [R2]

The reviewed announcement does not provide a complete country-by-country language and handset matrix, or establish activation on every eligible device. Do not assume Hindi support merely because India is listed, or mistake call-screening and translation language lists for Scam Detection requirements.

Samsung’s announcement also does not establish the same implementation or privacy behavior as Google’s Pixel features. For the separate Pixel experience, see [DECODISTA’s Pixel Scam Detection guide](https://decodista.com/articles/pixel-scam-detection-chat-gboard-privacy-2026). [R19]

## Warranty and Care

Samsung brings warranty details, diagnostics, repair estimates, Samsung Care+ and service booking into Settings where supported. Coverage information can include other Galaxy devices linked to your Samsung Account. [R12]

Availability is local: repair estimates depend on model and region, while Care+ eligibility, pricing, coverage and service fees vary. A menu entry does not mean every service is offered in your country or included free. [R13]

## Now Brief gets more customizable

New generative and editable cards let users tailor health, weather and video information. Samsung requires a Samsung Account; personalized briefings use personal information the user has consented to share for that purpose. Some functions need a connection, and support varies by market, language, model and app. Generated information can be inaccurate. [R17, R11]

Check the underlying weather, calendar or health app before acting on a summary that matters.

## Now Nudge

Samsung describes suggestions that help during conversations—for example, finding reservation details or saving a location while making plans. [R17]

Its current product-page footnote names Samsung Messages, Google Messages and Google Chat for message prompts. A Samsung Account is required, with regional and language restrictions; this is not a promise of support for every messaging app. [R4]

Samsung’s support guide shows activation under **Settings → Galaxy AI → Now nudge** on supported S26 devices. Check suggested destinations or calendar details before accepting an action. [R18]

## My FanCam

My FanCam reframes an already-recorded video around a selected person, keeping that person in focus through automated cropping and tracking. It requires Samsung Account sign-in and a video with a subject the feature can track; Samsung does not guarantee accurate results. [R17]

Review the exported clip for unwanted cuts or tracking changes. The description is not a promise of professional tracking or recovery of detail that the original recording never captured.

## Interpreter improvements

Samsung describes a Thread View for reviewing earlier translated exchanges, faster Conversation Mode and quicker playback with compatible Galaxy Buds. [R17]

An account is required; some languages need downloaded packs. Supported languages and features vary by market, carrier and device experience. The reviewed rollout notice does not provide a complete model-by-model compatibility matrix for these specific improvements. Translation accuracy is not guaranteed. [R11]

## Document Scan improvements

The update is intended to better handle curved book pages and folded corners, with improved Finger Eraser cleanup and large-screen layouts on compatible hardware. [R3]

Samsung’s detailed capture conditions still matter: the document should be rectangular with its corners visible, and overlapping fingers or other obstructions can prevent cleanup. Check names, amounts, signatures and page edges before treating a scan as a usable record. This is not a guarantee of perfect restoration. [R9]

## Voice Recorder changes

Samsung says summaries can use clearer structures, including headings and tables. [R3]

Transcript Assist’s transcription and summary functions require a connection and Samsung Account. Supported inputs are tied to the preinstalled Samsung Voice Recorder or recordings from Samsung Phone, Notes or Interpreter. Audio must be **under three hours**; summaries also need sufficient text. Language availability and recording support vary, and output may be inaccurate. [R11]

Keep the original recording and verify any important quotation against it.

## One UI 9’s lock-screen security change

Samsung India specifies stricter protection starting with **devices launched with One UI 9.0**. Its table escalates delays from one minute after five failed attempts to 24 hours after twelve; the **13th failed attempt permanently locks the device until a factory reset**. [R10]

That means a reset is needed to regain use, not that the hardware becomes unusable forever. Resetting erases local data; a backup matters. Know your PIN, pattern or password even if you normally use biometrics, and stop guessing when the phone warns about remaining attempts. [R10, R20]

The U.S. support page uses broader introductory wording, but it does not establish the policy for every older upgraded model. Do not extrapolate the India launch-device rule to all updates. Consult your model’s guidance; a permanent lock requiring reset should not be confused with a claim that every phone automatically erases itself at the threshold. [R20]

## Does One UI 9 run Android 17?

Yes. Samsung’s beta announcement identifies Android 17 as its base, and Samsung’s released Galaxy S26 FE documentation also describes One UI 9 running on Android 17. That corroborates the platform beyond beta wording. [R7, R14]

## How to check for One UI 9

Open **Settings → Software update → Check for updates**, as shown in Samsung’s U.S. guide. Other builds use **Download and install** or **System updates**; follow your device’s labels. [R6, R16]

Read the offered update’s version and installation notes. If it is absent, check Samsung Members and your carrier’s notice. A release announcement cannot establish delivery to your exact handset. Avoid treating a beta registration banner as a stable update offer.

## What to do before updating

Use this practical checklist:

1. **Back up important data.** Check that the backup completed and separately preserve essential files or app data you cannot afford to lose.
2. **Charge the phone.** Samsung’s general update guidance recommends at least 50% charge and an internet connection; some carriers require Wi-Fi. [R15]
3. **Check storage.** Use the update screen’s requirement; there is no verified single free-space figure for every model. [R15]
4. **Update apps.** Check both Galaxy Store and Play Store. [R16]
5. **Know your credentials.** Confirm your screen-lock credential and account recovery access before restarting. [R10, R20]
6. **Check work requirements.** Ask your administrator if a managed phone or essential work app needs certification.
7. **Read the local release notes.** Check carrier availability and security-policy changes that apply to your model.
8. **Allow uninterrupted time.** Follow the installer and do not force a shutdown during installation.

Afterward, test calls, mobile data and essential apps. Review available AI features and permission prompts; do not assume the update enabled or preserved every setting exactly as you prefer.

## Should you update immediately?

Consider installing when the official update is offered to your device, your backup is current, critical apps are compatible and you have time to check the result.

Consider waiting for clarification if your employer requires approval or a documented issue affects your exact model and essential workflow. A carrier or enterprise delay is also a reason to follow that channel’s instructions.

This is a decision framework, not a claim that One UI 9 is bug-free—or that everyone should delay security updates. Check the particular release offered to you.

## Features you may not get even after updating

Software version is only one requirement. Hardware, country, region, language, carrier, account state and installed apps can all affect availability. [R4, R5, R6]

Privacy Alerts has a specific model list. My FanCam needs a suitable video and account. Scam Detection is market-dependent. Now Brief depends on supported content and consent; Interpreter needs supported languages; Warranty and Care follows local service availability. Refer to the restrictions above rather than expecting identical features on every phone.

### Which Galaxy phones have One UI 9?

On supported models. Samsung lists specific devices and cautions that the feature may miss relevant factors. [R1]

### What is Security Brief?

It brings security information into Now Brief, subject to account and availability requirements. [R9]

No. Check model, region, language, account, app and hardware requirements. [R4, R5]
